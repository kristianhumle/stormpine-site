Reference-ready before you need it.

Executive references are asked for late and needed fast. Line them up while things are calm, so a final round that moves quickly never catches you flat-footed.

9 checksUpdated Aug 2026Tick as you go

The reference stage is where a strong process can wobble. It arrives at the end, when you are busy and the timeline is tight, and a scramble to find the right people, or a referee caught off guard, undoes weeks of good impressions. The fix is simple: prepare before you need it. Work through the list below and tick each item off.

Choose your referees

  • ✓Map three to five potential referees across different chapters of your careerA former chair or board member, a CEO you reported to, a peer, a direct report.
  • ✓Cover the competencies this role will probeMake sure between them they can speak to strategy, delivery, and how you lead people.
  • ✓Check for any conflicts or discretion issuesAvoid referees still inside a current employer if your search is confidential.

Prepare them

  • ✓Ask each one, properly, and confirm they are willingA quick call, not a surprise. Willing referees speak with warmth; ambushed ones sound flat.
  • ✓Brief them on the role and what mattersA short note on the position and the two or three themes you would love them to reinforce.
  • ✓Confirm current contact details and best way to reach themDirect line or personal email, and the times they are reachable.

Keep it current

  • ✓Note when you last spoke to each refereeA referee you have not contacted in two years needs a warm-up before you need the reference.
  • ✓Give the search consultant a clean, ready list when askedNames, roles, relationship to you, and contact details, in one place, on request.
  • ✓Thank them afterwards, and keep the relationship warmToday's referee is tomorrow's sponsor. Close the loop and stay in touch.
