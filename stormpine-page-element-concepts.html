A

Chips

Each audience as a bordered pill — more structured and scannable, still restrained.
Built for Experienced leaders Directors & VPs Board candidates Advisers & portfolio-builders

The idea

Gives each role a defined shape; reads as a clean set of tags.

Watch

Four pills can feel slightly "tag-cloud"; keep the borders subtle.

B

Icon + label

A small line icon per audience for quick decoding — echoing the product's icon language.
Built for Experienced leaders Directors & VPs Board candidates Advisers

The idea

Most visual/scannable; ties the strip to the product's iconography.

Watch

Icons risk feeling consumer-y for an executive audience — keep them thin and quiet.

C

Situation sentence

Reframe from job titles to the situations they're in — more sophisticated, on-positioning.

For the leader weighing a director role, building a board seat or advisory portfolio, and preparing for the move after this one.

The idea

Speaks to the moment, not the résumé line — the most executive, least "list-y".

Watch

Loses the quick who-it's-for scan; better if paired with the "Who it's for" section below.

D

Eyebrow + roles

A small "BUILT FOR" label above a clean row of roles — clearer hierarchy, very quiet.
Built for
experienced leaders·directors & VPs·board candidates·advisers & portfolio-builders

The idea

Separates the label from the roles for cleaner structure; keeps the restraint of "now".

Watch

Closest to current — a refinement more than a rethink.

E

Quiet one-liner

Drop the label; state it as one understated sentence. Maximum restraint.

For experienced leaders — directors, board candidates and advisers managing a small number of high-stakes moves.

The idea

The most discreet; disappears into the page and lets the hero model lead.

Watch

May be too quiet — easy to skip.

My take: for this audience, D (Eyebrow + roles) or C (Situation sentence) feel most executive; A (Chips) if you want more visible structure.
