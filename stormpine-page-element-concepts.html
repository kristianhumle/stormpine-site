Theme toggle

Productionheader utility cluster · every page

The light/dark switch in the header. A compact, deliberately quiet pill switch: a neutral track with a sliding thumb that carries a sun (light) or moon (dark) icon, so the control shows both its current state and what tapping it will do, without competing with the primary CTA. Built with role="switch" and aria-checked; the thumb icon and label update on toggle and on OS theme change.

NOW

Pill switch (sun / moon)

✓ live
Light
Dark

The idea

A switch reads as a two-state control at a glance, unlike a lone glyph where the icon can mean either "current mode" or "what you'll get". The moving thumb and the sun/moon icon make the state unambiguous, while a neutral track keeps it from reading as a call to action.

Behaviour

Thumb sits left with a sun in light mode and slides right with a moon in dark mode. Respects prefers-reduced-motion (no slide), is keyboard-focusable, and re-syncs if the OS theme changes.

Research · alternatives explored
PREV

Accent-filled pill

An earlier take on the switch: the track filled with the brand accent in dark mode. Toned down because a solid indigo pill sat at nearly the same visual weight as the "Start free" CTA beside it.
Light
Dark

Why we toned it down

The filled indigo track competed with the primary "Start free" button for attention. A theme toggle is a utility control, so it should recede, not read as a call to action.

Kept from it

The pill-switch shape, the sliding sun/moon thumb and the accessibility wiring; only the colour weight changed to a neutral track and a muted thumb.

ORIG

Glyph icon button

The original: a 34px button showing ☾ or ☀. Superseded; a single glyph is ambiguous about state vs. action.
☾

Why we moved on

Users cannot tell whether the moon means "you are in dark mode" or "switch to dark". There is no visible on/off affordance, and it looks like the other icon buttons rather than a control.

Kept from it

The sun/moon metaphor and the single-tap behaviour; only the shape changed.

Best practice this follows

A pill switch (or segmented control) rather than a bare icon is the common pattern across executive-grade SaaS (Linear, Vercel, Notion, GitHub settings), because it signals a binary state. We keep it compact for the header, use role="switch" and aria-checked for assistive tech, place the icon on the thumb so the metaphor moves with the state, and keep the track neutral so the control stays a quiet utility rather than competing with the primary CTA. Trade-off: an icon-in-thumb switch is a little wider than a glyph, so it stays in the same header utility cluster and the mobile menu carries the rest of the controls.

Hero

Production · Htop of every page

The frontpage hero models what Stormpine does rather than screenshotting it. We explored eight directions (A to H). Direction H, "living pursuits, different tempos", is confirmed and productionised as the live home hero; content pages (pricing, trust, legal) use a simpler centered variant of it. The research directions are kept below for the record.

LIVE

H · Living pursuits, different tempos

✓ live hero
S Stormpine ProductPricing

Manage your next career move with the context and clarity it deserves.

A private system for experienced leaders managing director, board and advisory pursuits. Keep the relationships, conversations, materials and next steps in one place, with grounded AI that assists you at every stage.

The idea

In then out, alive with activity. Context flows in (left, product icons); each pursuit orbits at its own pace and surfaces its real activity; executive clarity flows out (right).

The motion

Three orbits, desynced (~9 / 11 / 13s) so they never line up. As a dot passes a point, that activity, with its icon, pops up then fades.

Why it won

The fullest telling: what feeds in, the living pursuits of different lengths, and the judgement out. Product icons make it quick to read.

Do / never split

Productionintegrations · LinkedIn boundary

A two-column boundary statement: what a feature will do (green checks) beside what it will never do (coral crosses). It draws an explicit data boundary in the reader's eye, used for the LinkedIn self-import concept, and reusable anywhere a capability needs a clear "and not this" line.

Will / will never

✓ liveA framing pattern, not a new renderer type: two feature lists inside a band.

What it will do

  • ✓ Seed your profile on day one with your existing data.
  • ✓ Import your first-degree connections as contacts you already know.
  • ✓ Keep your own posts and activity as private signals.

What it will never do

  • ✕ Look up or enrich other people from a LinkedIn URL.
  • ✕ Monitor anyone else's job changes, posts or moves.
  • ✕ Scrape the network or buy scraped data from a broker.
